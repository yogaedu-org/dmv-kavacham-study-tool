How well it works so far

Well on the one voice tested, and unknown on every other. Here is the test and what it showed.

The planted-slip test

We took Day 5, verses 1 to 13, and damaged each in two ways at a random spot: two syllables left out, or two syllables replaced by the two that come six later. That made 26 slips. The 13 untouched takes were kept as clean controls, and Day 2 was the reference. Both checks were asked: which syllable do you mark most strongly?

What happened

Both put their strongest mark on the slip almost every time: 26 times out of 26 for the quick check and 25 out of 26 for the deeper check.

But a mark on the right syllable is not the same as a score that stands out. For the quick check, only 19 of the 26 slips scored higher than the highest score any clean take got. For the other seven you could not have told from the score alone that anything was wrong. That is why every mark in this book is a lead to listen to, not a verdict.

Look at each verse

In the pictures each verse has a clean take (hollow circle), an omission (round dot) and a substitution (diamond). The dashed line is the highest score a clean take got. A slip is easy to separate when its dot is far above the line.

What this can and cannot tell you

It can tell you how the method did on slips planted into one chanter's takes. It cannot tell you how it will do on your voice, or on a real mistake rather than a splice. The limits are in chapter 8.
