Hearing the verse as data

A computer does not hear syllables. It sees numbers: how big the sound is, a hundred times a second. This chapter shows the numbers behind one verse.

Three views of the same sound

  • The sound picture shows how the air pressure at the microphone changes over time. Tall is loud. It is the blue shape in the lower picture.
  • The loudness line is how loud the sound is in each hundredth of a second. It is the gold line.
  • Pauses are stretches of at least 0.3 seconds where the sound drops very quiet compared with the loud parts of the clip. They are shaded red. Quiet at the very start and end of a clip is not counted.

Where the syllable boundaries come from

The cells in the ribbon sit at the moments an automatic tool marked each syllable's start in the Day 2 recording. That is the tool's best estimate, not a person's decision. Chapter 3 measures how well those moments carry over to other takes.

Press play and the current syllable lights up. Press a syllable to hear from there, and see whether the pauses fall where you would breathe.

For the technically curious

The top picture is the waveform. The loudness line is the frame level in decibels, one value per 10 milliseconds. A pause is a run of 0.30 seconds or more where that level is more than 28 decibels below the clip's 95th percentile level; the earlier analysis then asked whether it sat inside a line or at the boundary between lines. The earlier run used 20 millisecond frames, so counts can differ slightly. The syllable moments come from CTC forced alignment with torchaudio MMS_FA on 20 millisecond frames (report 1, section 5; method, "Passes").
