Hearing the verse as data

A computer does not hear syllables. It sees numbers: how big the sound is, a hundred times a second. This chapter shows the numbers behind one verse.

Three views of the same sound

  • The waveform is the air pressure at the microphone over time. Tall is loud.
  • The loudness curve is the size of the sound in each slice of 10 milliseconds, in decibels. It is the gold line below the waveform.
  • Pauses are found by one rule from the earlier analysis: a stretch of 0.30 seconds or longer where the loudness is more than 28 decibels below the clip's 95th-percentile level. The earlier analysis then asked whether each one sat inside a line or at the boundary between lines. Quiet at the very start and end of a clip is not counted.

Source: pause rule, report 1 section 5 and method "Passes". The earlier run used 20 ms slices; this page uses 10 ms, so the counts can differ slightly.

Where the syllable boundaries come from

The cells in the ribbon sit at the moments a forced aligner placed each syllable in the Day 2 takes. That is an algorithm's estimate, not ground truth. Chapter 3 measures how well those moments carry over to other takes.

Source: method, pass A (CTC forced alignment, torchaudio MMS_FA, 20 ms frames).
