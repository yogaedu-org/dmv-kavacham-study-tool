What "correct" can mean for a chant

Ask three chanters whether a verse was chanted correctly and you may get three different questions. Here are the three a machine can work with, and the three it cannot.

Three things you can check

  • Which syllables. A verse is a fixed sequence of aksharas (syllables). Correct can mean every one is there, in order, and sounds like itself. The slips are an omission, a substitution (for example tha for ta or ś for s) and an insertion.
  • How long. Each syllable has a length and each line a rhythm. A pause in the wrong place, a rushed line or a held syllable all show up as timing. The earlier analysis flags a syllable at 0.4 times or less, or 2.0 times or more, of its usual length, and a pause of 0.30 seconds or longer.
  • What pitch. How the voice moves, measured against the key you chose, not against a fixed note. Only the movement is compared (chapter 4).

Sources: slip types, report 1 "Staged plan"; flag thresholds and pause rule, report 1 section 5, taken from the chant accuracy method.

What a machine can and cannot hear

It can compare your sound with a reference take, syllable by syllable, and say where it differs and how much evidence that rests on. It cannot know what you meant to chant, and it cannot know the reference is right: the method assumes the written verse is what the chanter intended. The speech models it borrows were trained on speech, not on chanting. And a difference that appears on most days may be the tool's habit or simply the way you say that syllable; only you can tell which. So every finding on these pages is a hypothesis until a listener listens.

Source: method, sections "What is hypothesis" and "Model-habit rule".

The evidence we start from

One chanter recorded all 14 verses on each of six days. Three automated listeners (a forced aligner, a letter decoder and Whisper) compared every take with the written verse, and the results were combined into a confidence for each line. Day 2 came out best.

These numbers are an estimate, not a measured probability. The weights were chosen by the analyst, not fitted to human judgements, because no human-labelled correct and incorrect takes exist. Day 1 verses 1 and 2 were cut wrongly in the recording and are not scored.

Source: method, sections "Confidence that a line was chanted as written" and "Score and recommendation", 2026-11-11. Data: chant.json, evidence.days.
