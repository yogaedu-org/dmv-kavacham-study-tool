What "correct" can mean for a chant

Ask three chanters whether a verse was chanted correctly and you may get three different questions. Here are the three a machine can work with, and what it cannot do.

Three things you can check

  • Which syllables. Every one is there, in order, and sounds like itself. The slips are an omission, a substitution (tha for ta, or ś for s) and an insertion.
  • How long. A pause in the wrong place, a rushed line or a held syllable all show up as timing.
  • What pitch. How the voice moves, measured against the key you chose, not against a fixed note (chapter 4).

What a machine can and cannot hear

It can compare your sound with a reference take, syllable by syllable, and say where it differs and how much evidence that rests on. It cannot know what you meant to chant, or that the reference is right: the written verse is assumed to be what the chanter intended. It was built on speech, not chanting. And a difference that shows up on most days may be the tool's habit or simply your way of saying that syllable; only you can tell. So every mark in this book is a hypothesis until a listener listens.

The evidence we start from

One chanter recorded all 14 verses on each of six days. Three automatic checks compared every take with the written verse, and the results were combined into a confidence for each line. Day 2 came out best, so it is the reference here. The scores are estimates, not measured probabilities.

For the technically curious

The three automatic checks were a forced aligner (torchaudio MMS_FA), a letter reader on the same pass, and faster-whisper large-v2. A syllable is flagged at 0.4 times or less, or 2.0 times or more, of its usual length. A pause is 0.30 seconds or longer. Day 1 verses 1 and 2 were cut wrongly in the recording and are not scored. Day 2: mean 89.6 with every line at 70 or more in all 14 verses; Day 5 and Day 6: 85.5; Day 1: 86.1 over 11 scored verses; Day 3: 85.3; Day 4: 80.2. The weights behind the scores were chosen by the analyst, not fitted, because no human-labelled correct and incorrect takes exist (method, 2026-11-11; slip types and thresholds, report 1).
