Lining up two takes

Two takes of the same verse never run at the same speed. Before you can compare them syllable by syllable, you have to say which moment of one matches which moment of the other.

What warping is

Picture two rubber bands, each marked with the syllables of the verse. One is stretched a little more in the middle than the other. To compare them you stretch and squeeze each part until the marks sit on top of each other. A method called DTW does that by computer: for every moment of the reference it looks at the tone of the sound and finds the cheapest way to match each moment to one in the other take, keeping the order.

Once that is known, the syllable moments of the reference can be carried onto your take, and each syllable measured: how long you held it, how well it matched, how loud it was, where its pitch sat.

How accurate is the lining up?

The Day 2 syllable moments were carried onto 63 other takes and compared with where the automatic tool had marked each syllable there. The typical error was about 9 thousandths of a second, and 99 times in 100 it was under a tenth of a second. Each line below is one take.

What this can and cannot tell you

The reference moments are an automatic estimate, so this measures how two methods agree, not truth. All 63 takes are by the same chanter as the reference. A different voice has not been tested: the tone of the voice may make lining up worse (a hypothesis).

For the technically curious

The tone of the sound is a short list of numbers called MFCCs, 12 per 10 millisecond slice on this page; the report's method adds their changes over time (deltas), which this page's aligner does not, so the accuracy below belongs to the report's version. Across 2,025 syllable starts in 63 takes the median error was 9 ms, the 90th percentile 27 ms and 99.2 % were within 100 ms. The speed, 0.22 s per verse pair, was measured with NumPy, not in the browser. A syllable is marked when its length is 1.6 times or more, or 0.625 times or less, of the take's overall tempo; the earlier analysis used 2.0 and 0.4. That threshold comes from the aligner's code and is not fitted. Sources: report 1, "Local evidence"; raw numbers dtw_probe.json.
