Pitch

Pitch is how high or low the voice is at each moment. In a chant it is the tune: where the voice rests, and how it rises and falls on a syllable.

How pitch is tracked

A pitch tracker looks for a repeating cycle in a short slice of sound. The time between repeats gives the pitch. A slice with no clear repeat, such as a breath, a hard consonant or silence, is left blank, which is why the lines in the ribbon break.

Recording matters too. A phone or browser may switch on automatic volume levelling or noise removal, and the report suspects these flatten sustained pitch (hypothesis; one discussion thread reports level drops on sustained tones). That is why Lab 1 asks the browser to switch them off, and then shows what it actually did, because it may ignore the request (MDN).

Cents, and why only the movement counts

A cent is a hundredth of a semitone, so 1200 cents is one octave. Each take's pitch is measured as cents above its own typical pitch. That way, chanting the same tune a little higher or lower, or in another voice, is not counted as an error; only the shape of the tune is compared (report 1, section 5).

See it on two real takes

Indigo is the reference and gold is the other take, drawn on the ribbon and again below it. A syllable gets a pitch mark when its pitch differs by 150 cents or more after the take's overall offset is removed, and enough of it was voiced for that to mean something. The 150 is from the tool's code and is a hypothesis, not a fitted value.

What this can and cannot tell you

It can show where two takes by the same voice differ in the shape of the tune. It cannot yet be trusted for a different voice: that is untested (chapter 8). It cannot hear a pitch the tracker could not find, so short or breathy syllables are left unmarked.

For the technically curious

The tracker on this page compares a slice with a delayed copy of itself (autocorrelation), searching 70 to 400 hertz; it comes with the audio side page's aligner. The report's candidates were pitchy (McLeod method, MIT, 4.1.0) and, if pitchy jitters on low chant (a hypothesis), SwiftF0 (MIT, 0.3.0). pitchfinder (GPL-3.0) and essentia.js (AGPL-3.0) are unfit for this MIT tool. Contours are warped onto the reference with the alignment from chapter 3 and folded into plus or minus 600 cents so an octave jump is not a huge difference. Sources: report 1, sections 1 and 5.
