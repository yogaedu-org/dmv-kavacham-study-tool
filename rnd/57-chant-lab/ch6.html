How well it works so far

The honest answer is: well on the one voice tested, and unknown on every other. Here is the test, the numbers, every limit we know of, and the test that is still missing.

The planted-slip test

We took Day 5, verses 1 to 13, and damaged each in two ways at a random position: two syllables left out (an omission), or two syllables replaced by the two that come six later (a substitution). That made 26 slips. The 13 untouched takes were kept as clean controls, and the reference was Day 2. The question for each tier: does the highest-scoring syllable fall within one before to two after the slip?

In plain words: both tiers put their top flag on the slip almost every time (26 of 26 and 25 of 26). But only 19 of the 26 planted slips scored higher than the worst clean take for tier 1, so for the other 7 you could not have told from the score alone that anything was wrong. That is why every flag on these pages is a lead to listen to, not a verdict.

Source: report 1, "Local evidence", measured 2026-11-12; per-verse numbers in dtw_probe.json and zipa_gop_probe.json.

Every limit we know of

  • One voice. The reference and every test take are the same chanter. Cross-speaker scoring is untested for both tiers (hypothesis that tier 2 is more robust to a different voice, since tier 1's features carry the voice's colour).
  • Hard splices. The planted slips leave sharp edges that a real slip does not, which probably makes them easier to find.
  • Reference moments are an algorithm. The syllable boundaries come from a forced aligner, not from a person marking them.
  • Tool habits. The aligner's catch-all class won every frame on one clip (585 of 585), and Whisper large-v2 looped on two clips (a looped clip's result is void).
  • Edges. Ignore the first and last syllable unless the clip starts or ends mid-sound: one clean take spiked at 6.87 on its first syllable.
  • No false-alarm rate. No human-labelled clean takes exist, so how often a clean take is flagged has not been measured.
  • The earlier smoke test. Five slips were spliced into Day 2 clips and scored: four were found and the line's confidence fell well below 70 (for example 91 to 32 for an omission); a repeated stretch was seen but placed several syllables away and kept a high score (91 to 91). Five clips is a smoke test, not a recall rate.
  • Not built or not measured. The browser version of tier 2's audio features does not exist yet and must match the Python one; the browser speed of tier 2 is not measured; thresholds on this site's pages are hypotheses, not fitted.
  • Storage on iPhone. Safari deletes a site's stored data after 7 days without a visit (web.dev), so recordings must be exported.

Sources: report 1 "Local evidence", section 7 and "Top risk"; method "Passes" and "Confidence" (calibration).

The biggest unknown

Another voice. Tier 1 compares the colour of the sound, which carries the voice; tier 2 should be more robust but already merges ś with ṣ and drops aspiration on the one voice tested. Both statements are hypothesis until the live test below.

The live test that is still missing

Three chanters, at least one who is not the author and one of the other sex, each chant five verses twice: once clean, and once from a card that names one slip in advance. The slips: an omission, tha for ta, ś for s, an insertion, a one-second pause, and a rushed half-length line. Report how many were found of how many, by type, and how many false flags a clean verse got. Fix the thresholds on two chanters and test on the third. Lab 3 on this site is a first, easy version of the same tally.

What this can and cannot tell you

It can tell you how the method did on slips planted into one chanter's takes. It cannot tell you how it will do on your voice, or on a real mistake rather than a splice.
